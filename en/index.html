The offline logger for Wavelog

Log where you operate. Sync when the network is back.

Tideline is the open-source app for radio amateurs: log QSOs offline on a summit, in a park or in a contest, and sync them with your own Wavelog – traceable, QSO by QSO.

All platforms

Open source (MIT) · no account with us · no tracking · iOS, iPadOS, Android, macOS, Windows · Current version 1.0.0

Four ideas everything is built on

  1. 1 / 4

    Offline is normal

    Everything except the sync itself works with no connection – including DXCC lookups, reference lists and “worked before” hints.

  2. 2 / 4

    Sync you can follow

    Every QSO shows its state: local, queued, uploading, synced, conflict or rejected. When the server objects, the app says why in plain language. Nothing is silently lost or duplicated.

  3. 3 / 4

    Built for the field

    Glove mode, sunlight theme, night red, battery saver and keep-screen-on – plus full use with a screen reader and a keyboard.

  4. 4 / 4

    Secure and private by design

    No telemetry, no ads. The API token lives only in the operating system's secure store, with least privilege.

Features – by situation

What Tideline does depends on where you are sitting. Everything listed here is in version 1.0.

Activation (SOTA · POTA · WWFF)

Summit, park, forest – no signal.

  • You download the reference lists yourself from the official sources, then search offline by name or distance.
  • Progress toward a valid activation, also as text: POTA 10, SOTA 4 and WWFF 44 QSOs (default rules, checked in October 2026).
  • Park-to-park and summit-to-summit: enter the other station's reference per QSO.
  • MY_SOTA_REF, MY_POTA_REF and MY_WWFF_REF go into the QSO automatically.
  • ADIF export of a single activation.

Contest

Keyboard first, speed over decoration.

  • Keyboard entry in a dense layout; serial numbers that never repeat.
  • Dupe checks and super check partial from the MASTER.SCP you download yourself.
  • Live rates and multipliers; edit without leaving contest mode.
  • Bundled definitions: CQ WW, CQ WPX, ARRL DX, IARU HF, DARC WAG and generic templates.
  • Cabrillo export and Wavelog contest sessions (Wavelog 3.2 or newer).

Field Day & portable operation

Many QSOs, little time, changing operators.

  • Fast Log Entry: type QSOs as shorthand, see how each line was read, log them all at once (Wavelog SimpleFLE dialect).
  • Field mode in one switch: sunlight theme, glove mode, battery saver, keep screen on.
  • Generic contest templates for serial-number or exchange contests. There is no dedicated Field Day definition.
  • Several Wavelog accounts and station profiles, switchable on the log screen.

Station at home

The calm part: maintain, check, tidy.

  • “Worked before” hints from your Wavelog log; DXCC lookup offline.
  • Callsign directory built from your QSO history, with your own notes (local only).
  • ADIF import and export, backups, optional app lock with biometrics.
  • Free up space: remove local copies of QSOs already synced. Nothing is deleted on Wavelog.
  • On Mac and Windows it behaves like a desktop program: sidebar, menu bar, keyboard shortcuts.

Planned – not included yetplanned

  • Device-to-device sync over local Wi-Fi, no internet
  • WSJT-X listener on the desktop
  • Rules editor for activation counting, and two-fer activations
  • Background sync on Android

Accessibility: part of the work, not an afterthought

The target is WCAG 2.2 AA. Contrast is checked in tests, not by eye.

  • Text ≥ 4.5:1, controls ≥ 3:1, sunlight theme ≥ 7:1 – tested automatically.
  • Text scales up to 200% with no clipped content.
  • Status is never colour alone: always an icon and text.
  • Screen readers spell callsigns letter by letter (“D O 1 H O Z”).
  • Full keyboard use with a visible focus; motion follows “reduce motion”.
  • VoiceOver and TalkBack hand tests are done for 1.0, according to the changelog.

Privacy: a promise the app keeps

Tideline collects no data about you. There is no telemetry, no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service and no tracker. The developers never receive your data.

  • Your log stays on your device – and on your own Wavelog server.
  • It connects only to your Wavelog and – when you press “Download” – to the visible reference-list sources.
  • No location, no camera. You type the locator yourself.
  • The API token lives only in the Keychain, Android Keystore or Windows protected storage.
  • The database and backups are excluded from iCloud, iTunes and Android backups.

To be straight about it: Tideline encrypts nothing of its own. Protection on the device comes from the operating system; exported backups are plain files – keep them safe.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Wavelog?

To sync, yes: Wavelog 3.1.0 or newer with API v2. To try the app there is a built-in demo account that needs no server and no network.

Does Tideline really work with no signal?

Yes. Logging, DXCC lookup, reference and “worked before” hints all run locally. Only the sync itself needs a connection – it starts as soon as one is there.

What happens if Wavelog rejects a QSO?

The QSO is kept with the state “rejected”, and the app explains why in plain language. Nothing is silently dropped or replaced.

Does it cost anything?

Tideline is open source under the MIT licence. On the App Store the app is free.

Is my data encrypted?

The app does no encryption of its own (only TLS in transit and the OS secure store for the token). The database is protected by the operating system; backups and exports are unencrypted.

Is there a Field Day definition?

No. There are generic templates for serial-number and exchange contests; Fast Log Entry and field mode help with speed.

What about WSJT-X?

A WSJT-X listener for the desktop is planned but not included yet.

How does Tideline differ from other apps?

There are several good tools. Tideline focuses on one codebase for five platforms, offline-first and a visible sync state per QSO. Try what fits your way of operating.
